<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I joined the haycut team with David and Chris. We were going to take a green hay cut from a very species rich bank on the reserve and add it to the seeded meadow.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The main idea was to just cut the patches with sparse grass, and just enough to get the seed heads.  No need to go very low, as we might do during winter habitat management.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It took a bit of concentration to keep the blade high and also try to cut in one sweeping right to left motion. By mid morning tea break we had collected around 7 bags to be transported to the other field.  Meanwhile, the rest of the team completed a hand harvest of certain species. There was a lot of lady&#8217;s bedstraw, devil&#8217;s bit scabious, cowslip, ribwort plantain, st John&#8217;s wort and many others.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After lunch we spread the remaining hay over the seeded field.  It&#8217;s quite an intensive method to cut and transport, but the benefit is to spread some of that very local DNA to other parts of the reserve.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">By 2pm we had pretty much finished and headed back home through Stroud.</p>
