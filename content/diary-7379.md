<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Pete had provided some sheep netting which would give some knee high protection from muntjac and roe deer &#8211; at least from the important main stem.  We already had a few stakes and managed to cobble together some tools to try and get the stakes in the ground.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The ground is so hard, it makes it very hard to knock posts in.  We didn&#8217;t have a wrecking bar sadly which would have been ideal to make a substantial hole.  But we did have a crow bar to fashion at least something we could drive into.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the end we managed to get two stakes in and fixed the netting with cable ties on the first tree.  It wasn&#8217;t really terribly secure and will need knocking in further when the ground is softer.  But they are certainly a little more deer proof that the bare tree this morning.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After a tea break we managed to get the second tree done a lot quicker than the first.  This happens quite often &#8211; the first task takes ages while you figure out the bets way to do it, but then the next one is a lot smoother.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We had lunch in the end meadow, which will be the scene of the scything task next week.  </p>
