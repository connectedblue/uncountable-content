<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We planted the hedge along the drive to Fromebridge Mill back in January 2023, and spent <a href="https://diary.uncountable.uk/2023/04/mulching-hedgerow/" data-type="post" data-id="84">many work parties since</a> looking after it.  The task today with <a href="https://www.gloucestershirewildlifetrust.co.uk/volunteer">Gloucestershire Wildlife Trust</a> was to lay the hedge along the left hand side in the field.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We split into two teams, and one group headed off to pollard willow near the pipeline, which would come in very handy towards the end of the day.  The rest of us armed with bill hooks, silkies, axes and loppers to start at the far end.  A couple started with the bramble trimming to make room for the first end lay, while we got on our knees to start hacking at the base of the young stems.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As ever with these type of tasks, the first hour is spent trying different techniques and getting into a rhythm.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In general, with only three year old stems, the task was much, much easier than the laying we did with <a href="https://diary.uncountable.uk/2025/12/ramping-up-the-laying/" data-type="post" data-id="6710">30+ year old trees</a> on the reserve last year.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The bill hook and small axe were the best tools for shaving and splitting the bark before laying.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Last week a hedge expert visited and gave some tips about making the split as close to the ground as possible and to try and make it as long a possible, giving a larger surface area for new shoots.  We also tried to avoid square hinges, instead aiming for a smooth  bend as close to the ground as possible.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After tea and cake break, we really accelerated.  Five were ahead making the initial cuts, while Suzi, Clare and I laid them.  We found that by gently pulling the top of the plant downwards, a few final axe cuts released enough tension to get the stem completely horizonal.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Meanwhile Tom and Pete took the collected willow from the other team and staked the hedge from the end.  The long pollarded branches weaved around the stakes and really pushed the laid hedges against the ground.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Overall, we made a fabulous start and should have it finished in a couple more sessions.  With it&#8217;s proximity to the driveway, we&#8217;ll be able to keep a regular eye on progress during the next growing season.</p>
