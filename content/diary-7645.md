<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Our regular seasonal trip to a <a href="https://www.cotswolds-nl.org.uk/looking-after/our-grasslands-projects/glorious-cotswolds-grasslands/">Glorious Grasslands</a> favourite.  We&#8217;ve been working for many years at this site, which has fly orchids, duke of burgundy and all round fabulous habitat.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I was checking with Chris how many times we&#8217;d been here &#8211; not only twice a season for at least the last five habitat management seasons, but we&#8217;ve also done seed harvesting several times, as well as <a href="https://diary.uncountable.uk/2024/06/a-wildflower-walk/" data-type="post" data-id="3326">a summer walk</a>.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I spent the first period before tea break with the fly orchid team.  The challenge today was to deal with the encroaching wild clematis, which covers a lot of the site.  We particularly wanted to ensure that a particular area where fly orchids thrive had as much removed as possible.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It turned out to be quite tricky and fiddly.  The clematis was quite thin and whispy, so cutting it wouldn&#8217;t make a lot of difference.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"> It was also somewhat complicated by the numerous ash shoots and the thick leaf cover.  But while raking around, we quickly identified multiple fly orchid rosettes which could be marked with sticks and targeted more precisely.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After break, I switched to the brush cutter and cut out paths in the Duke of Burgundy area.  Towards the end of the afternoon, I then moved over to the bank edges on the main track.  This site is one that doesn&#8217;t get grazing, so it&#8217;s really an annual job to keep it cut and raked off for the next growing season.</p>
