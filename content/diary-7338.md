<p class="wp-block-paragraph">My first harvesting of the year with <a href="https://www.cotswolds-nl.org.uk/looking-after/our-grasslands-projects/glorious-cotswolds-grasslands/">Glorious Grasslands</a>, and we returned to the same place where we had been <a href="https://diary.uncountable.uk/2024/07/sweltering-harvest/" data-type="post" data-id="3454">two years ago to the day</a>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One of the memorable features of the last harvest here was the huge swirl of hawksbeard seed which really irritated the eyes, and marked the point where safety glasses became a standard part of my harvest PPE.  However, today the hawksbeard had mostly blown away.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There were five of us today and two harvesters.  It was pretty full on from the off, especially as a huge amount of grass was being picked up.  We experimented with raising the brush (although we didn&#8217;t want to miss the yellow rattle) and also lowering the revs on the harvester motor.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The latter seemed to work most effectively and we then started to get more manageable loads.  Tom thought about maybe going to a couple of other nearby fields, but on closer inspection the key species in those fields that we would have wanted hadn&#8217;t set seed yet.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I learned something new today &#8211; Red bartsia is also parasitic on host grasses.  Yellow rattle gets all the glory in this department, but we had some in this parcel today.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Baking hot day today, so we were mighty relieved to have finished by half two and have an air conditioned drive home.</p>
