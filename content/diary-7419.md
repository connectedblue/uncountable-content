<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This morning was the quarterly meeting of the <a href="https://discoverdursley.org.uk/">Discover Dursley</a> group.  A smaller meeting due to a lot of people on summer holidays, but productive as there is always so much to catch up on with all the events and activities around the town.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the afternoon, I headed over to the cricket club for a Wilder Frome citizen science event.  This is a project of the <a href="https://www.severnriverstrust.com/project/frome-fish-pass-restoring-river-connections/">Severn Rivers Trust</a> to remove 100 or so fish barriers along the Frome.   The focus of the day was to showcase all the opportunities for people to get involved in citizen science in the catchment.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I went to assist Kai&#8217;a in demonstrating riverfly monitoring to groups.  There was a nice easy footbridge access to the Frome next to the cricket pitch and we could set up the sample trays on the bridge itself.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We planned for two groups.  The first was around 10 people and 4 in the second group.  This includes some children who are always good to engage in the activity.  As expected, the river levels were low and we didn&#8217;t have a whole lot to look at &#8211; plenty of gammarus and cased caddis, along with the odd olive.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Becky from Severn Rivers Trust also demonstrated phosphate and nitrate testing which was very interesting.  I&#8217;ve not seen that before and we had a good discussion about the data collection and where it goes.  They collect and use the data mainly to evidence their own projects.  Sadly there isn&#8217;t a co-ordinated national effort to collect phosphate baseline data.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After we finished with the demo there was a walk around the river with Chloe, John and Jacqui.  Chloe is managing fish passage through all the barriers and explained some of the projects and solutions they have coming up.  We went to look at the three way weir just to the side of the cricket ground which was a good example of what they have to deal with.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">John from <a href="https://www.gloucestershirewildlifetrust.co.uk/volunteer">Gloucestershire Wildlife Trust</a> talked about a number of projects he&#8217;s undertaking with landowners around the river to improve biodiversity in general.  Jacqui is a poet and doing a research project on poetry walks along waterways.  She read out her poems at different points along the route.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">All in all, a very enjoyable afternoon and evening and I learned a lot more about the projects along the Frome.</p>
