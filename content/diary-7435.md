<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A smaller group today with <a href="https://www.gloucestershirewildlifetrust.co.uk/volunteer">Gloucestershire Wildlife Trust</a>, perhaps because of the baking temperatures expected in the middle of the day.  So instead of the planned hay cut of the corky fruited meadow, we decided to focus on the other end by the A38 gate.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The rest of the team took scythes and rakes to clear the overgrown towpath, while I took the brush cutter to the landscaped corner which we attempted to seed earlier in the year, but which was now entirely taken over by dominant species and hardly any grass.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We had great success on the strip on the other side of the fence, which was seeded last winter and has been fenced off to cattle (and humans).</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So the main job today was to cut low, remove the nutrient and prepare for when the ground is hopefully wetter in October to seed.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The cut was pretty straightforward &#8211; I managed to make long parallel strips of arisings which could be more easily forked over to the habitat pile.  There were patches of area that had grass underneath the more dominant plants, so there is hope for the strategy.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So a successful day and the ground prepared for the next phase later in the year.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We&#8217;ll give it a go and see what happens.  My main fear would be that the already established perennials will crowd out the finer meadow grasses again, but maybe a winter sowing will help.  There&#8217;s also the issue that it&#8217;s unfenced, so will be potentially walked over by dogs and humans when it&#8217;s trying to establish.</p>
